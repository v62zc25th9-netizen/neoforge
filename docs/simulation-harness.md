# Simulating the whole console

Can we run a NeoForge ROM through a full Verilog model of the Neo Geo, and
sweep the cartridge's access time until it breaks — with no hardware and no
copyrighted data?

**Yes.** This is the scoping result. `[MEASURED: 2026-09-12]`

The harness is not built. What follows is a path with no unknown blockers left
in it, which is what scoping was for.

---

## Why bother

`docs/measurement-cart.md` was demoted from prerequisite to optional
contribution once we found the known-good access-time envelope. Simulation is
what replaced it: the same sweep, for free, with nothing at risk.

It cannot establish the real limit — the model is not the machine. What it can
do is check our understanding of the read cycle, show whether 120 ns has margin,
and catch design errors before any board exists.

## What we found

### The model very nearly compiles as it stands

`neogeodev/NeoGeoFPGA-sim` builds under Icarus Verilog with everything resolved
**except the CPU**, once two things are dealt with:

- Several modules live in files whose names do not match them — `rom_sps2` is in
  `ROMs/rom_sp.v`, `ram_68k` in `RAMs/m68kram.v`, `lspc_timer` in
  `Video/timer.v`, `jedi_lut` in `Audio/jedi.v`. Icarus's `-y` library search
  maps module names to filenames, so these must be passed explicitly. Not a bug,
  just a build-invocation detail that costs an hour to work out.
- **`System/neo_c1.v` has a duplicate declaration.** `nPORT_ZONE` is declared as
  an `output` in the port list and again as a `wire` in the body. Icarus rejects
  it outright, so **the model as shipped does not compile with Icarus at all.**
  Deleting the redundant `wire` line fixes it. `[MEASURED: 2026-09-12]`

That is the second bug we have found in this repository, after the `BANKSEL`
width truncation in `Cartridge/zmc.v` (see `docs/serializer.md`). Both are
one-line fixes and both should go upstream.

### The one real blocker is the CPU

`CPUs/cpu_68k.v` instantiates `tg68`, which is **VHDL** (`tg68k.vhd`). Icarus
Verilog cannot simulate VHDL, so the system cannot be run as shipped by anyone
without a mixed-language simulator.

### And the fix is an improvement

**fx68k** — Jorge Cwik's cycle-accurate 68000 in SystemVerilog — lints clean
under **Verilator 5** with `--timing`, given the usual warning suppressions.
`[MEASURED: 2026-09-12]`

That matters more than just unblocking us:

- **Verilator 5 honours `#delay`.** Timing control arrived in Verilator 5.0, and
  our whole experiment is changing `#120` and watching what happens. Verilator 4
  would have been useless here — it ignores delays entirely.
- **fx68k is microcode-accurate**, where TG68K is not. One of the stated caveats
  on this whole approach was that the CPU model was not cycle-exact. Replacing it
  removes that caveat rather than working around it.

## The path

1. Get the NeoGeoFPGA-sim model through Verilator's checks. It passes Icarus;
   Verilator is stricter and will find more. Unknown size, but bounded — the
   model is not large and the issues so far have been trivial.
2. Write a wrapper adapting fx68k's bus signals to what `cpu_68k.v` presents to
   the rest of the model.
3. Convert ROM images to `$readmemh` text. The model wants 24 data files.
4. Run, confirm boot, then sweep the P ROM's `#120`.

### Every input is open

The model's data files include `rom_sp-s2_fast.txt`, `rom_sfix.txt` and
`rom_l0.txt` — the system BIOS, its fix tiles, and the zoom lookup table. Those
are the files that would normally make this impossible to publish.

They all have open equivalents in ngdevkit:

| Model wants | We supply |
|---|---|
| `rom_sp-s2` | **nullbios**, LGPL-3.0-or-later |
| `rom_sfix` | nullbios's fix tiles |
| `rom_l0` | generated by nullbios's `zoom-rom.py` |
| `data_p1`, `s1`, `m1`, `c1`, `c2`, `v1` | **our own** `rom/` and `rom-sound/` |
| `raminit_*` | zero-filled |

We already use nullbios without thinking about it: the `aes.zip` and
`neogeo.zip` our ROM builds copy out of ngdevkit's share directory *are*
nullbios.

**This is the point, not a workaround.** A harness needing a BIOS dump and a
commercial cartridge produces a number nobody can check. One that runs on
nullbios and a ROM in this repository produces one anyone can rerun. See
`contributing.md`.

## What it will and will not prove

**Will:** whether our reading of the read cycle is right; whether 120 ns has
margin; where the *model* puts the edge; and whether cartridge-side decode and
wait-state logic behaves as we think — which is the part NeoForge must
reproduce.

**Will not:** the real limit. The model is schematic-derived and encodes its
author's understanding, which is part of what we would be testing. Two of us
agreeing is not silicon.

**And nullbios is not the real BIOS.** It does not do the cartridge probe, the
eye-catcher sequence, or the full Z80 handshake. The harness says more about
steady-state bus timing than about whether a marginal cartridge survives
startup.

## The number that does not come from here

How much margin 120 ns represents is **arithmetic, not simulation**: the MC68000
read cycle at 12 MHz with 0 and 1 wait states, from the datasheet's AC
characteristics.

We tried to extract that table from two scans and could not — the read/write
cycle specifications are in foldout pages that did not survive OCR. **Unresolved,
and deliberately not guessed at.** Anyone with the MC68000 user manual to hand
can close it in five minutes, and it tells us whether SNK chose 120 ns because
the bus demanded it or because it was what was cheap.
